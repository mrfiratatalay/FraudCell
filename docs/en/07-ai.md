# 07. AI

What makes FraudCell different is how the AI and people work together: the model finds risky transactions, the
analyst decides, and the customer tells us whether the decision was right. The model is measured against that answer.

Full model card: [MODEL_CARD.md](../../BACKEND/src/Ai/fraudcell-ai/MODEL_CARD.md).

## Two models

| Model | Algorithm | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | HistGradientBoosting + sigmoid calibration | Score from 0 to 1 |
| Fraud type | RandomForest (300 trees) | Stolen card, account takeover, money laundering, suspicious behaviour |

Calibration matters: a score of 0.90 should really mean "very likely", because the thresholds (0.40 / 0.90) are applied
directly to that score.

## Data

- 10,000 synthetic Turkish card and wallet transactions from about 1,250 simulated customers, with a fixed seed
  ([synthetic_data.py](../../BACKEND/src/Ai/fraudcell-ai/app/ml/synthetic_data.py)).
- 11% fraud. Labels are sampled from a logistic function of the features, so the classes overlap and the metrics aren't
  inflated.
- 120 hand-written Turkish scenarios ("late at night, from abroad, repeated large card payments on a new device", for
  example) are **not** used for training; they are an independent acceptance set.
- The data is committed, and a test checks the file matches the generator byte for byte.

## Features

15 columns ([features.py](../../BACKEND/src/Ai/fraudcell-ai/app/ml/features.py)):

- **Amount:** the amount and how far it deviates from the customer's average.
- **Time:** the hour and whether it's night (Istanbul time).
- **Novelty:** abroad, new device, new recipient.
- **Velocity:** transactions in the last 10 minutes and the last 24 hours.
- **Transaction type.**

The AI service can't connect to the Transaction database, so it computes velocity and novelty from **its own**
prediction history. That was the price of keeping the service boundary.

**The clock bug:** The container ran in UTC, so a transaction at 02:00 Istanbul time reached the model as 23:00, and the
"night" feature was wrong. I converted the time to Istanbul explicitly and pinned it with a test.

## Metrics, honestly

On the test set, at the 0.40 threshold:

| PR-AUC | Precision | Recall | F1 | False positive rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.609 | 0.715 | **0.521** | 0.603 | 0.026 |

- Recall 0.52 means the model catches about half of the frauds. At this threshold I chose to keep false alarms low; the
  cost is the cases it misses.
- On the Turkish scenarios: 100% of legitimate ones passed, 47% of fraud ones were flagged, and the type was right 87% of
  the time. Suspicious behaviour is the weakest type at 33%.
- This limit is also in [12 FAQ and limits](12-faq-and-limits.md); I don't hide it in interviews.

## Explanations

Every score comes with **reason codes** such as "first time seen device" or "10× the usual amount"
([reason_codes.py](../../BACKEND/src/Ai/fraudcell-ai/app/ml/reason_codes.py)). They are not the model's internals;
they are rule-based, derived from the features, and written so an analyst can read them. With `GEMINI_API_KEY` set, an
LLM turns the same facts into a fluent paragraph; without it, a template writes the paragraph. The LLM only writes
prose; it never touches the score or the decision
([explanation.py](../../BACKEND/src/Ai/fraudcell-ai/app/ml/explanation.py)).

## Feedback loop

1. When a supervisor corrects the AI's type or risk level, `case.fraud_type.overridden` /
   `case.risk_level.overridden` arrives.
2. When the analyst decides, `case.decision.made` arrives together with the customer's answer.
3. The AI writes these to `prediction_feedback` ([feedback.py](../../BACKEND/src/Ai/fraudcell-ai/app/ml/feedback.py))
   and computes precision and recall per type.
4. The "model accuracy" card on the supervisor's dashboard shows them.

Only graded rows count, so unanswered cases don't inflate the rates.

## When the AI is down

If the AI service fails, the transaction is still recorded. With no result within 5 seconds, the case opens with an
unknown risk in the manual queue for a supervisor to assign. CI's `demo.py ai-down` step stops the AI container and
checks this on every PR.

## Model lifecycle

- The model is trained from the committed data while the Docker image is built; artifacts are not committed.
- `model_versions` and `model_bundles` record versions and checksums. If a checksum doesn't match, the service reports
  "not ready" and fails its health check.
- Splitting out the training dependencies cut the image from 1.72 GB to 700 MB.
